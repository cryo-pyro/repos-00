What you're proposing is an interesting metaphorical framing, but it is not how the Greek mythological texts themselves describe it. In Hesiod's Theogony, Gaia isn't said to be created by a process in any scientific sense — she simply appears after Chaos. Scholars stress that both are primordial beings who come into existence in sequence, each fundamental in themselves.

PhaseEntityHesiodic Reading
0ChaosYawning gap / void — not "entropy" per se
1GaiaPrimordial Earth — appears after Chaos, not produced by it
2OuranosSky born from Gaia — boundary and consort
3TitansGaia–Ouranos offspring; raw generational force
4OlympiansBorn from Titans — legitimate successors, not "optimized code"

That said, the modern symbolic mapping is not illegitimate. Myth has always been an updating mechanism. Homer reshaped inherited myth for narrative force. Hesiod systematized it for cosmology. Lovelock re-weaponized Gaia for Earth system science. The real question is not whether the update is permitted — it's whether it deepens explanatory power or merely decorates it.

RISK 1 Over-physicalizing flattens the mythic register. Hesiod was describing power succession, generational violence, legitimacy. Reduce it purely to entropy gradients and you lose psychological depth and political allegory.
RISK 2 Teleology sneaks in. Greek myth is cyclical and tragic — Titans aren't wrong, Olympians aren't morally superior. If you modernize it as optimization, you risk importing a Silicon Valley progress narrative into a fundamentally tragic cosmology.

Are you treating the Olympians as stable equilibrium — or as temporary attractors in a dynamical system?

The second reading makes your entire framework far more powerful. And much more Greek.
