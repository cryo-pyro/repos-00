## II
Ah, you're proposing a conceptual synonymy between loss, altitude, and temperature as measures of the same underlying thing—some kind of "distance from ground state" or "degree of excitation."

Let me push back: they're not synonymous, even metaphorically. They measure different dimensions:

**Temperature** in physics is about the *distribution* of energy states—how spread out the system is across possibilities. High temperature means lots of variance, exploration, accessible microstates. It's about disorder and traversability.

**Altitude** (potential energy) is about *position in a gradient*. You're literally higher in a field, storing energy that could convert to kinetic. It's deterministic positioning, not statistical spread.

**Loss** is about *error relative to a target*. It's inherently teleological—it only makes sense if there's a goal state you're trying to reach. High loss means "far from where you should be."

In mania, you could argue:
- High temperature (yes): huge variance in thought/behavior, rapid state transitions
- High altitude (maybe): elevated energy, but potential for what? Crash?
- High loss (questionable): loss relative to *what target*? Functioning? Happiness? The manic person often feels they're winning, not erring.

These three concepts only collapse into synonymy if you assume:
1. There's a single optimal ground state (questionable for complex systems)
2. Distance from it can be measured the same way across energetic, spatial, and error dimensions
3. The system is *trying* to minimize something

Living systems don't necessarily minimize anything. They persist, they cycle, they generate novelty. The bipolar cycle might not be "trying to solve" for anything—it might just *be* the attractor.
